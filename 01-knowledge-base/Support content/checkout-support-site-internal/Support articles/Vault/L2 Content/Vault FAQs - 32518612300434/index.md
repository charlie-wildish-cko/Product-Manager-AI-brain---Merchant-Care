**When to use this article **

Here you'll find answers to common questions about the Vault product.

## GENERAL 🔒

Can we modify Vault Export files (e.g., changing "MASTERCARD" to "MASTER") or re-export only specific failed records?

No. We cannot manually modify the files or change the data format.

- 
**Why we cannot change it:**

  - 
**⚠️ Automation:** We no longer have access to manually export or edit files; the process is now automated.

  - 
**Standardization:** "MASTERCARD" is the correct, official scheme name as received in the metadata from Mastercard.

  - 
**Technical Limitations:** We are not permitted to modify files, and we cannot easily alter the UI to toggle between formats like "MASTER" and "MASTERCARD".

- 
**Resolution for the Merchant:**

  - 
**The Issue:** The requirement for "MASTER" usually stems from a specific configuration or truncation choice made by the merchant or a different PSP.

  - 
**The Fix:** Since "MASTERCARD" is the standard value, the merchant must update their system (or their receiving PSP's system) to accept the full value rather than the truncated version.

How long do ephemeral tokens (tok_xxx) last?

You must use these single-use tokens within 15 minutes of creation; otherwise, they are permanently deleted.What is the difference between a "token" and an "instrument"?

You should use the term "token" for ephemeral, single-use data with a 15-minute lifespan, while an "instrument" (SRC_ ID) refers to the long-term token stored in the vault for recurring payments.What error code should you expect if you try to use an expired or already used token?

You will receive a 422 error code, which helps you distinguish between an expired token and other merchant configuration errors.What should you do if you cannot confirm a merchant's stored public key hash?

You should escalate the request to Vault L3 so they can check DynamoDB to verify the public key's existence.

## NETWORK TOKEN QUESTIONS (NTs) 🎫

What are Network Tokens (NTs)?

Network tokens replace sensitive information with surrogate values. This means you replace sensitive card details like your long card number, expiry date, and CVV with a token.How do Network Tokens reduce fraud?

By using a network token instead of raw card details throughout the payment chain, you risk less exposure of sensitive data to third parties. This prevents bad actors from intercepting card details for unauthorised use.Why do Network Tokens improve authorisation rates?

Because card schemes and issuers work together to create these tokens, they are more likely to recognise the payment as a safe transaction. This trust leads to higher authorisation and acceptance rates.Are Network Tokens generated in real-time?

Yes, they are generated in real-time as a cardholder enters their details or when a card is stored on file.What is a major pain point for merchants who don't use Network Tokens?

You may lose money when stored card details are not updated, as transactions using expired cards will fail. Additionally, card schemes often charge higher fees if you do not use network tokens.How do Network Tokens benefit subscription-based businesses?

For your following transactions, cardholders don't need to re-enter their CVV, which optimises the payment experience and reduces the chance of basket abandonment.What is a 'TRID' and why is it important?

A TRID is a Token Requestor ID assigned once you successfully onboard with card schemes like Visa or Mastercard. It allows you to begin requesting network tokens.Can a Network Token be stolen and used at a different store?

No, network tokens are uniquely bound to individual merchants. This security measure ensures a bad actor cannot steal a token from one store and use it elsewhere.What is the difference between asynchronous and synchronous provisioning?

In asynchronous provisioning (the default), your first payment is processed with raw card details while the token is created in the background for future use. In synchronous provisioning, the first payment is held until the token is generated, ensuring you benefit from lower scheme fees immediately.What is a cryptogram in the context of NTs?

A cryptogram is a one-time security layer generated by the card network that is sent alongside the network token to provide an additional level of security during a transaction.How long does a network token cryptogram last?

A cryptogram typically lasts for 15 minutes. While you can use the same cryptogram for a second payment within that timeframe, you cannot request multiple cryptograms for a single transaction.How do Network Tokens (NTs) differ from standard PCI or Vault tokens?

The card scheme issues your network tokens rather than your acquirer or payment provider. This makes them interoperable across the entire payment ecosystem, whereas PCI tokens are usually specific to your provider's vault.Will your customers need to do anything when their card expires?

No, because the card schemes automatically update the underlying card details, your customers do not need to take any action or re-enter details when they receive a replacement card.What is the benefit of using Network Tokens for your SCA strategy?

You can use NTs to help reduce the scope of Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) requirements. This cuts payment friction by reducing the number of "challenge" or "step-up" authentications your customers face.Does using Network Tokens save you money on transaction fees?

Yes, major schemes like Visa often offer lower interchange fees for card-not-present transactions when you use network tokens because the risk of fraud is lower.What is the difference between 'Initialization' and 'Provisioning' in the NT process?

Initialization is when your customer enters their details at checkout. Provisioning is the step where you share those details with your provider, who then requests the token from the card scheme.How does Checkout.com help you manage the lifecycle of a token?

Checkout.com handles token lifecycle events from the schemes and issuers on your behalf, ensuring your network tokens remain up to date without manual intervention from your team.Can you opt out of requesting a Network Token for a specific payment?

Yes, if you do not want to request provisioning for a specific transaction, you can set the provision_network_token field to false in your payment request.What happens if a Network Token payment is declined due to a system error?

If a payment is declined with certain codes (like a timeout or system malfunction), the system will automatically retry the payment using the full card details (FPAN) to ensure the sale is not lost.Can you use Checkout.com Network Tokens with other processors?

Yes, you can request and extract your network tokens and cryptograms to use them with your chosen acquirer or processor, provided you have the correct permissions.How can you monitor the success of your token requests?

You can view your results in the Dashboard under 'Vault Network tokens', where you can see failure reasons and success rates for each card scheme.What is 'Involuntary Churn' and how do NTs help you prevent it?

Involuntary churn happens when a customer's subscription fails because their card expired. Since your tokens are automatically updated by the schemes, your recurring payments continue seamlessly.Is the CVV required for every Network Token transaction?

Once you have a network token stored on file, your customers don't need to re-enter their CVV for subsequent transactions, which helps you reduce basket abandonment.How do you receive updates when a card is replaced?

You should subscribe to the network token webhooks. This ensures your system receives notifications whenever a card scheme updates the state of a token.What platforms generate Network Tokens for Visa and Mastercard?

Visa provisions them via the Visa Token Service (VTS), and Mastercard uses the Mastercard Digital Enablement Service (MDES).

## APPLE PAY AND GOOGLE PAY 📱

How do you decrypt Apple Pay and Google Pay payloads?

You use a processing certificate to decrypt the payload, which allows you to access the Device Primary Account Number (DPAN) and Secure Authentication Data (SAD).Why might you receive a "token data invalid" error during Apple Pay/Google Pay setup?

This usually happens because you cannot decrypt the payload, often due to certificate management issues such as using an incorrect domain or certificate.How can you reduce the risk of decryption errors for merchants?

You can encourage merchants to use "Flow" onboarding, where your team manages the certificates, rather than the merchant managing them manually.How do Apple Pay/Google Pay DPANs differ from standard Network Tokens?

While both provide security, Apple Pay tokens are generated via the wallet provider and do not interact with your standard network token service, though schemes like Visa/Mastercard recognise both as network tokens.Are Google Pay transactions always processed via DPAN?

Not always; while phone-based Google Pay usually uses 3DS and a DPAN, Google Pay via the web often passes the actual card number (FPAN) instead.

## VAULT EXPORTS AND COMPLIANCE 📂

How are Vault export files secured for PCI DSS compliance?

You cannot see decrypted PCI-sensitive data; the system encrypts the export file using a public PGP key provided by the merchant or PSP at the time of download.What should you check if a PSP reports missing instruments in a Vault export?

You should check BigQuery for "deleted" events related to those specific source IDs, as the export query is designed to exclude deleted instruments.
