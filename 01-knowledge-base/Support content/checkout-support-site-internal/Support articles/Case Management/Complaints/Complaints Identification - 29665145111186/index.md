 **Introduction**

This article provides clear guidance on how to quickly spot and classify a complaint based on the language used. It also helps you differentiate between a formal complaint and a minor inquiry. 

Use this document to help you identify when a merchant message is considered a formal complaint, according to Checkout.com's policy. This guide is for anyone who interacts with our merchants or prospects, including our Commercial, Disputes, Merchant Care, RevOps, Risk, and Underwriting teams. 

Your role is to serve as the initial point of contact, ensure the complaint is correctly identified, the required information is gathered and the issue is referred to our dedicated Complaints team.**General Questions****What is the definition of a complaint?**

A **complaint** is **any expression of dissatisfaction** (spoken or written) from a **Checkout customer** (or someone acting on their behalf) **about our financial services/products**, alleging **actual or potential**:

- financial loss,

- 
**material** distress, or

- 
**material** inconvenience.

**Who is considered a complainant?**

A complainant is a Checkout.com customer, which is a business with a signed services agreement. Prospects (who have not yet signed a contract) are not formal complainants under our policy, but any dissatisfaction they express should be treated seriously. If a partner, payment service provider, or consultant complains on behalf of a signed merchant, it is also considered a complaint.**How can I quickly check if something is a complaint?**

Use this simple checklist to determine if a message is likely a complaint:

- 
**Dissatisfaction?** Is the customer unhappy or disappointed with something?

- 
**About us?** Is it about Checkout.com's services, products, onboarding, fees, or staff conduct?

- 
**Impact (actual or potential)?** Do they claim financial loss, delays, missed sales, stress, or significant inconvenience?

- 
**Are they a customer?** Is the person a current customer or their representative?

If you answer **Yes** to all four questions, it is likely a complaint. If you are ever unsure, it is safest to treat it as a complaint and route it accordingly.**Specific Questions****What are the key language cues for a complaint?**

While not an exhaustive list, these are common words and phrases that indicate a formal complaint:

- 
**Explicit complaint words:** "I want to make a complaint," "Please escalate," or "I want this reviewed by a manager".

- 
**Harm, loss, or remedy seeking:** Words and phrases such as "lost revenue," "overcharged," "material impact," "compensation," "refund," or "fee waiver".

- 
**Unacceptable service or conduct:** "This is unacceptable," "not fit for purpose," or "incorrect advice".

- 
**Regulatory or legal triggers:** Mentions of "regulator," "ombudsman," or "legal action" or "breach of contract".

**How do I classify a complaint from a prospect versus a customer?**

**Formal Complaint:** This comes from an existing customer who expresses dissatisfaction and claims they've been negatively affected. These must be officially logged and counted in our formal complaint statistics.

**Prospect Dissatisfaction:** This comes from a potential customer** **(a prospect). While it should be logged as "prospect dissatisfaction" and escalated internally, it is **not** considered a formal complaint and is not included in our official statistics.**What information should I capture for a complaint?**

When capturing a complaint, you must include the following details:

- The merchant's Client ID

- The channel, date, and time of the communication (e.g., email, ticket, or call)

- A summary of the issue and a transcript

- The alleged impact (e.g., financial loss, operational disruption)

- Any remedy the merchant has requested

- Any immediate actions you have taken

**PSD vs Non-PSD**

PSD governs **payment-service complaints** and requires a **final reply within 15 business days** (or **35** in exceptional cases with a holding reply). Use PSD when the issue is about **executing or enabling payments**; otherwise treat as **Non-PSD** under the local regime.

### **PSD Examples**

Classify as **PSD** when the complaint concerns a Checkout payment service in UK/EU/EEA. Typical buckets:

- Card acquiring / processing - auth/capture/settlement/recon/scheme messaging, e.g., “Auth declines spiked Friday; we lost €12k.”

- Payouts / settlement / funding - withheld or delayed funds, settlement errors, FX on settlement, e.g., “Payouts due yesterday didn’t land; we can’t pay suppliers.”

- Refunds / returns - delayed/failed/duplicate refunds, e.g., “Refunds aren’t reaching customers; chargebacks are rising.”

- Chargebacks / representment - missed deadlines or evidence errors causing loss, e.g., “You missed the representment deadline; we lost the case and fees.”

- Availability / incidents (incl. 3-D Secure) - outages/routing failures blocking transactions, e.g., “API outage blocked transactions for 2 hours.”

### **Non-PSD Examples**

Treat as **Non-PSD** when it’s **not about executing/enabling payments**:

- Pre-contract sales/onboarding (prospects) - e.g., “Sales promised X before we signed.”

- Account management/service quality with no payment impact - e.g., “Our AM hasn’t replied this week.”

- Reporting/UI with no payment-execution impact - e.g., “The dashboard is confusing.”

- Pure information/data/privacy requests - e.g., “Please send the pricing sheet / PCI doc.”

**Merchant Care Referral**

When you have correctly identified a complaint, from an eligible complainant, the next step is to refer the case over to our dedicated complaints team within Merchant Care. 

For internal Zendesk users, utilise the `Care to Complaints` macro for internal transfer, for everyone outside of Zendesk, email the case to [complaints@checkout.com](mailto:complaints@checkout.com). Make sure to include the following information in your referral:

- The merchant's Client ID

- The channel, date, and time of the communication (e.g., email, ticket, or call)

- A summary of the issue and a transcript including PSD or non-PSD

- The alleged impact (e.g., financial loss, operational disruption)

- Any remedy the merchant has requested

- Any immediate actions you have taken

 ⚠️ If an email is sent to complaints@checkout.com from Japanese issuers, JCA(Japan Credit Association), or JCB local, then those should be identified as Japan complaints immediately.**Complaints Chatbot**
