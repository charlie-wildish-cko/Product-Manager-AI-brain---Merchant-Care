| **Which are the merchants that need to be enrolled for the PCI DSS?** | All merchants that accept, process, store, or transmit credit card information are required to comply with PCI DSS This includes businesses of all sizes and across all industries, as long as they handle payment card transactions. However, the specific requirements and the level of compliance vary depending on the merchant’s size and the volume of transactions they process annually. |
| **Should all CKO merchants by default, be enrolled for PCI DSS?** | All CKO merchants are enrolled for PCI DSS by default because it is a fundamental requirement for any entity involved in processing card payments. Whether a merchant is small or large, compliance ensures protection against data breaches, liability, and regulatory penalties, and is typically a contractual obligation with their payment provider. |
| **Are there any exceptions for merchants not to be enrolled for PCI DSS?** | Exceptions or reduced scope can apply to smaller merchants, those that use third-party service providers, or merchants using technologies that minimize their involvement with sensitive data. However, they must still meet some level of PCI DSS compliance based on their specific circumstances. While some merchants may not need to complete the full PCI DSS compliance process, no merchant is fully exempt if they handle credit card transactions in any way. Even in cases where exceptions or reduced requirements apply, merchants must still validate compliance annually and ensure that their handling of cardholder data is secure. |

| **What does PCI DSS protect?** | PCI DSS protects cardholder data (PAN, cardholder name, service code, and expiration date) and sensitive authentication data (full track data from the magnetic stripe or equivalent data on the chip, CAV2/CVC2/CVV2/CID/CVN2, and PIN/PIN block) from a payment card representing a PCI SSC Participating Payment Brand (American Express, Discover, JCB, Mastercard, UnionPay, or Visa). Bank account information, such as branch identification numbers, bank account numbers, sort codes, routing numbers, and so on, is not considered payment card data and is not subject to the PCI DSS. However, if a bank account number is also a PAN or contains the PAN, PCI DSS is applicable. It should be mentioned that up to 10 PAN digits may be included in some bank account numbers, such as the IBAN (International Bank Account Number). PCI DSS is applicable if an IBAN or any other account number has more PAN digits than the truncation formats specified by that specific payment brand (refer to FAQ 1091). It is highly advised that an account number containing PAN be protected, even in cases where PCI DSS does not apply, to stop unauthorised individuals from deriving the entire PAN from the account number. |

| **What are the consequences if a merchant doesn’t validate their PCI compliance?** | The most important consequence is that they may be at risk of account data compromise. PCI compliance helps merchants protect their business from hackers and data thieves. It also limits liability in the event of an unavoidable breach. Merchants who experience a data breach and are not PCI compliant are subject to fines, penalties, and associated costs resulting from the breach. These can exceed $100,000, and the reputational costs could be even worse. |
| **Do merchants need to renew their PCI DSS compliance?** | Yes, a merchant needs to renew their PCI DSS compliance regularly to ensure they continue to meet the required security standards. The frequency and process for renewal depend on the merchant's level of PCI compliance, which is determined by the volume of credit card transactions they handle annually. Key Points on PCI DSS Renewal:   1. Annual Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ):    1. Most merchants are required to complete a Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ) annually. This is a set of questions that helps merchants assess their security practices and ensure they meet PCI DSS requirements    2. The SAQ type depends on how the merchant processes credit card transactions (e.g., SAQ A, SAQ D)    2. Quarterly Network Scans:   - If the merchant’s systems are connected to the internet, quarterly vulnerability scans conducted by an Approved Scanning Vendor (ASV) are required. These scans check for security weaknesses that could be exploited by attackers.   3. Annual On-site Assessment (for Level 1 Merchants):   - Merchants who handle a very high volume of transactions (Level 1 merchants) must undergo an annual on-site assessment conducted by a Qualified Security Assessor (QSA). This involves a thorough review of the merchant’s security policies, procedures, and technical controls.   4. Attestation of Compliance:   - After completing the required assessments and scans, merchants must submit an Attestation of Compliance (AOC) to their acquiring bank or payment processor. This document confirms that they meet PCI DSS requirements. |
