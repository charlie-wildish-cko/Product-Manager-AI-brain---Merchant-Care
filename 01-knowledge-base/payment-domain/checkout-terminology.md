# Checkout.com Terminology

> Official definitions for Checkout.com product, operational, and payment terms. Source: https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/terminology (last updated January 28, 2026)

## A

**ABU** (Mastercard Automatic Billing Updater) – A Mastercard service that helps merchants get updated information for cards that expire or are replaced.

**acceptance rate** – The percentage of successful payments that are ready to be captured out of all requested payments. Calculated by dividing accepted payments by requested payments.

**accepted payments** – The total number of accepted or partially accepted payments in the specified time frame and currency.

**ACH** (automated clearing house) – A US-based network that facilitates electronic funds transfers between banks.

**acquirer** – An entity that processes card payments for merchants, working with card schemes and issuers to settle transactions.

**acquirer fee** – The fee merchants pay to their acquirer for processing and handling card payments. A breakdown type field in reconciliation reports.

**acquiring bank** – A financial institution that handles a merchant's account so they can accept credit or debit card payments.

**ACS** (Access Control Server) – The server operated on the issuer's domain to authenticate cardholders during 3D Secure transactions.

**AFT** (Account Funding Transaction) – A transaction type used to transfer funds from a card account to non-merchant accounts, such as topping up a prepaid card.

**AML** (anti-money laundering) – Regulations and policies designed to prevent money laundering and detect illicit financial activities within legitimate financial channels.

**APM** (alternative payment methods) – Payment options beyond traditional credit and debit cards, such as digital wallets (e.g. PayPal, Apple Pay), bank transfers, and BNPL services.

**ARN** (Acquirer Reference Number) – A unique number assigned to a payment by the acquiring bank, used to trace the payment throughout the payment processing ecosystem.

**authentication rate** – The percentage of payments successfully authenticated by the issuer or payment network, out of those for which authentication was initiated.

**authorization rate** – The percentage of successfully authorized payments out of all payments submitted to the card scheme for authorization.

**AVS** (Address Verification Service) – A fraud prevention tool used to verify a customer's address against the cardholder's billing address held by the issuer in card-not-present transactions.

## B

**bank payout** – A payout transaction made to a bank account.

**BIC** (Bank Identifier Code) – A standardized international code that banks use to identify themselves in financial transactions. Also known as a SWIFT code.

**BIN** (Bank Identification Number) – The first six to eight digits of a payment card number, identifying the financial institution that issued the card.

**blocked payments** – Payments declined due to risk, either before or after authorization.

**bp** (basis point) – A unit of measurement in finance. One basis point = 0.01% (1/100th of 1%).

**business day** – A working day when financial institutions are open for processing transactions. Also known as a working day.

## C

**CAID** (Card Acceptor ID) – A unique number assigned to a merchant by their acquiring bank. Also known as a merchant ID (MID).

**capture rate** – The percentage of successfully captured payments that were accepted. Calculated by dividing captured payments by accepted payments.

**captured payment** – A payment that has been captured and becomes revenue available to the merchant.

**card account updater** – A service provided by card schemes that helps merchants automatically update card details for their customers.

**card scheme** – A payment network that defines rules and guidelines for payments and settles transactions (e.g. Visa, Mastercard).

**card token** – A secure, temporary reference to card details used for transactions instead of exposing sensitive cardholder data.

**cascading** – A transaction processing method that retries declined transactions with multiple acquirers until successful authorization is obtained.

**CAVV** (cardholder authentication verification value) – A value generated during 3D Secure authentication and returned to the merchant as part of the transaction process.

**chargeback** – A customer-initiated reversal of funds for a purchase, typically facilitated through their bank or payment service provider.

**chargeback ratio** – The percentage of total transactions that result in a chargeback, used to assess merchant risk level and compliance with card scheme rules.

**chargeback threshold** – A set percentage of a merchant's total transaction volume, after which chargebacks are flagged for review or penalties incurred.

**CIT** (customer-initiated transaction) – A payment where the customer actively initiates the transfer of funds to a merchant.

**client account** – An account created for a business to use Checkout.com's payment processing and other services.

**CNP** (card not present) – A payment transaction where the cardholder does not physically present the card, commonly used for e-commerce.

**CPD** (central processing date) – The date on which the card scheme processed a transaction.

**CSS** (card scheme screening) – A risk-checking tool provided by card schemes (e.g. Mastercard's MATCH, Visa's VMSS) used to assess merchants.

**CVV** (card verification value) – A 3–4 digit security code on payment cards used to validate card-not-present transactions.

## D

**decline code** – A short code (usually two digits) that indicates why a payment was declined.

**dispute** – When a customer questions the validity of a transaction with their bank or credit card issuer, leading to an investigation and potential reversal.

## E

**ECI** (electronic commerce indicator) – A code indicating the level of security used in a transaction when the cardholder provides payment details.

**EMI** (electronic money institution) – A financial organization authorized to issue and manage electronic money for online transactions.

**entity** – A legally distinct organization or business unit associated with a merchant. Used to separate functional or operational units within a merchant's ecosystem.

**entity segment** – Represents a combination of brand/website, business category, or location/market region where a payment originated. Each segment must be associated with a single entity.

## F

**FCA** (Financial Conduct Authority) – The UK regulatory authority overseeing financial services.

**fraud report** – A report relating to fraudulent activity, including reports received from card schemes (e.g. Visa TC40, Mastercard SAFE).

**FX rate** (foreign exchange) – The exchange rate at which one currency is traded for another during international transactions.

**FXP** (FX payouts) – A service for converting and paying out funds in foreign currencies.

## G

**GDPR** (General Data Protection Regulation) – An EU regulation designed to protect personal data and privacy.

**gross revenue** – For an acquirer: interchange and scheme fees plus the acquirer premium.

## H

**hard decline** – A transaction rejection that requires the issuer or cardholder to resolve the issue before retrying. Contrast with soft decline.

**Hosted Payment Pages** – A pre-built, secure payment interface hosted by Checkout.com where customers enter their payment details.

## I

**IBAN** (international bank account number) – A standardized international format for identifying bank accounts across countries.

**IC++** (interchange plus plus) – A pricing model where credit card processing costs are split into: interchange, card scheme fees, and Checkout.com processing fees.

**interchange fees** – Fees merchants pay to card schemes and issuers for processing credit and debit card transactions.

**issuer / issuing bank** – A financial institution that provides payment cards to customers on behalf of card schemes like Visa or Mastercard, and manages transaction authorization.

**issuing BIN** – The first six to eight digits of a payment card number, identifying the financial institution that issued the card. Equivalent to BIN.

## K

**KYB** (know your business) – A due diligence process designed to prevent money laundering and assess business legitimacy.

**KYC** (know your customer) – The process of verifying a customer's identity to prevent fraud and ensure regulatory compliance.

## L

**LPM** (local payment method) – A payment method popular in a specific region (e.g. iDEAL in the Netherlands).

## M

**marketplace** – A platform allowing multiple sellers to sell under a unified system, handling payments, settlements, and disputes.

**MCC** (merchant category code) – A four-digit code classifying a merchant's business based on the type of goods or services they provide.

**merchant** – A business that accepts payments for goods or services via Checkout.com.

**MID** (merchant ID) – A unique number assigned to a merchant by their acquiring bank. Also known as CAID.

**MIT** (merchant-initiated transaction) – A payment initiated by the merchant without the customer actively participating (e.g. recurring payments, installments, account top-ups).

**MoR** (Merchant of Record) – The entity legally responsible for processing payments and managing customer disputes, acting as the seller to the cardholder.

**MPT** (marketplace transfer) – The movement of funds between internal accounts within the platform before payouts are finalized.

**MSA** (Merchant Service Agreement) – A binding contract between Checkout.com and the merchant defining terms of service and responsibilities.

## N

**natural refund rate** – The total volume of refunded payments (partial or full) out of all captured payments in the specified time frame.

**net revenue** – For an acquirer: the acquirer premium.

**network token** – A unique digital identifier that replaces sensitive card details during payment transactions. Tokens are automatically generated by card schemes.

## O

**OCT** (original credit transaction) – A type of transaction enabling direct fund transfers to a cardholder's account (e.g. Visa Direct, Mastercard MoneySend).

**operational funding release** – When Checkout.com releases funds from your Operational balance to your Available balance.

**operational funding reservation** – A temporary hold on funds to ensure sufficient balance for operational expenses.

## P

**PAN** (primary account number) – The full, unique card number found on payment cards.

**PAR** (payment account reference) – A unique identifier for a PAN, shared across the physical card and all associated network tokens (e.g. Apple Pay, Google Pay). Enables linking all transactions to the same account.

**partial capture** – A payment that has been partially captured as part of a series of multiple captures or a single partial capture.

**PayFac** (payment facilitator) – An entity that contracts with a payment service provider to offer payment processing to sub-merchants. PayFacs assume liability for their sub-merchants' accounts.

**payin** – An incoming payment into an account or wallet.

**payment gateway** – A service that authorizes and processes electronic payments for online or traditional businesses.

**payment ID** – The unique Checkout.com identifier of a payment.

**payment method** – The way the customer pays for goods or services. Card payments are categorized by card scheme; APMs are categorized by individual method.

**payment processor** – A company that facilitates secure funds transfers between a merchant and a customer's bank.

**payment reference** – The description displayed on a merchant's bank statement for payments processed by Checkout.com.

**payment request** – A formal instruction sent by a merchant to request funds. The total number of payment requests received and processed by Checkout.com in the specified time frame and currency.

**payment volume** – The total monetary value of transactions processed over a specific period.

**payout** – A card or bank transaction paid to a beneficiary. Checkout.com's Funds Pooling solution enables merchants to process payouts even when a sub-account does not have sufficient funds.

**payout rejected** – A payout that Checkout.com rejected (e.g. due to a failed sanctions screening).

**payout returned** – A payout returned by Checkout.com's banking partners (e.g. due to incorrect beneficiary bank details).

**PCI DSS** (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) – A global security standard ensuring secure storage and transmission of cardholder data to reduce fraud.

**PIF** (payment instruction form) – A contractual document used to capture changes to a merchant's bank account details, particularly when settlements are paid to a legal entity other than the one on the MSA/MAF.

**PJF** (payment out of jurisdiction form) – A document required when funds are settled to a merchant's bank account located outside the merchant's jurisdiction.

**PoS** (point of sale) – A physical location where a cardholder completes a transaction.

**processing channel** – A set of configurations that defines how payments are processed for each of a merchant's legal entities. An entity can have more than one processing channel.

**PSD2** (Payment Services Directive 2) – A European regulation aimed at increasing payment security and fostering innovation. Outlines Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) requirements.

**PSP** (payment service provider) – A third-party entity that facilitates payment processing services for merchants. Checkout.com is a PSP.

## R

**reconciliation** – The process of ensuring that transaction records in the payment system align with bank and financial records.

**recurring transaction** – A transaction scheduled to recur regularly, as agreed between the merchant and customer (e.g. a monthly subscription).

**refund** – A transaction reversal that returns funds to the customer after payment. Can be full or partial.

**remittance** – The transfer of funds from one party to another, typically for payment or settlement.

**remittance currency** – The currency in which the merchant receives funds.

**remittance date** – The business day on which the remittance occurs, based on the remittance cycle and threshold defined in the pricing schedule.

**reported fraud-to-sales ratio** – The ratio of reported fraud value to gross sales volume, calculated monthly.

**response code** – A code indicating the outcome of a payment, sent back from the issuer to the payment processor after a payment is attempted.

**reversal** – When funds for a transaction are reversed back to the sender, usually initiated by the merchant to cancel or correct a previously authorized or settled payment.

**risk-declined payment** – A payment flagged and declined by Checkout.com's risk systems based on high-risk patterns or fraud indicators.

**rolling reserve** – A reserve of merchant funds held by Checkout.com, typically in the remittance currency, for 28 weeks. Usually ranges from 5–10% of each charge, assessed daily.

## S

**sandbox** – A controlled testing environment where changes can be tested before going into production, isolated from the production environment.

**SBP** (subscription billing platform) – A system enabling merchants to manage and automate recurring billing for their products or services.

**SCA** (Strong Customer Authentication) – An EU regulation mandating additional security checks to reduce fraud and secure online payments (contained within PSD2). Requires multi-factor authentication: something you know, something you have, something you are.

**settlement** – The process of transferring funds to a merchant's account after a payment is authorized and captured. Can be gross (full amount) or net (fees deducted).

**settlement currency** – The currency in which a settlement is paid by the card scheme.

**Smart Dunning** – A Checkout.com solution that improves payment recovery by predicting and scheduling retries at optimal times using machine learning and real-time network alerts.

**soft decline** – A declined transaction that may succeed on a subsequent attempt. Contrast with hard decline.

**SoR** (Seller of Record) – The entity legally identified as the seller in a transaction, which sells directly to the consumer.

**spot refund rate** – The percentage of refunded payments relative to total captured payments in the specified time frame.

**sub-account** – A secondary account under a Checkout.com client account used to manage and allocate funds with greater granularity. Each sub-account typically holds funds in a single currency.

**sub-entity** – A child of an entity. Represents branches, subsidiaries, clients, or specific business lines associated with the main merchant.

## T

**TDM** (transaction distribution management) – When transactions are redirected to a different acquirer if the initial acquirer declines.

**token** – A randomly generated string of characters substituting for a PAN (the actual 16-digit card number). Protects sensitive payment information.

**tokenization** – The process of replacing sensitive payment data with a secure, non-sensitive, unique token.

**TPA** (third-party acquirer) – A third-party processor that partners with Checkout.com to provide payment processing services in certain regions (e.g. US and MENA).

**transaction ID** – The unique Checkout.com identifier for a transaction.

## U

**UBO** (ultimate beneficial owner) – A natural person who ultimately owns or controls an entity, or on whose behalf transactions are performed.

**uplift** – In the Dashboard, an increase in a specific metric caused by a particular action or treatment. Used to measure the incremental impact of an intervention on payment KPIs.

## V

**VAU** (Visa Account Updater) – A Visa service that helps merchants obtain updated information for cards that expire or are replaced.

**VCR** (Visa Claims Resolution) – A system provided by Visa for resolving disputes.

**virtual card** – A digital-only payment card that functions like a physical card but exists only in electronic form.

**virtual terminal** – An online system enabling merchants to manually process card-not-present (CNP) transactions.

**voided payment** – A payment that was voided after authorization, but before funds were captured.

## W

**webhook** – An automated notification Checkout.com sends merchants when triggered by a specific event.

## Z

**zero-dollar authorization** – A zero amount authorization request used to verify cardholder payment information without billing the customer.

## Other

**2FA** (two-factor authentication) – An added layer of security requiring two methods of identity verification. A form of MFA.

**3DS** (3D Secure) – An authentication protocol that enhances security for online card payments while increasing the likelihood of a frictionless transaction. Exemptions can be requested for eligible transactions under PSD2.
