# Checkout Goal Setting Playbook

Your guide to setting, cascading, and tracking goals from Company to Individual level.

This Playbook walks through how to set clear goals at every level of Checkout, cascade them effectively, and measure outcomes using the WHAT/HOW framework. It provides practical examples, tips, templates, and guidance to help leaders coach and empower employees to achieve measurable impact.

By following this Playbook, leaders and employees will be able to:

* Set goals that are clear, measurable, and actionable
* Understand how their work contributes to company priorities
* Use a consistent language for goals across the organisation
* Conduct fair, evidence-based performance evaluations
* Embed a culture of clarity, accountability, and measurable outcomes

## Why This Playbook Exists

Setting goals is not just a box to tick — it's the backbone of a performance-driven culture. At Checkout, we want every employee to understand:

* What matters most to the company
* How their work contributes to success
* How performance will be measured and rewarded

This Playbook is designed to:

* Provide a step-by-step approach to setting goals at every level
* Give leaders practical tools and examples
* Enable a fair, transparent, and measurable performance culture

By following this Playbook, you will:

* Understand and utilise the same goal-setting language across the company
* Understand the throughline between Company and Individual goals
* Be able to coach and support your teams in achieving outcomes
* Ensure performance reviews are objective, data-driven, and fair

**Cultural North Star:** Clarity, Accountability, Feedback, Measurable Outcomes

## Why Goal Setting Matters at Checkout

Goals are not just administrative tasks — they are the bridge between strategy and execution. Strong goal-setting enables:

* **Clarity:** Everyone knows what matters most and what success looks like
* **Alignment:** Each person's work contributes to the company's priorities
* **Accountability:** Ownership is clear at every level
* **Fair Performance Management:** Outcomes are measurable and evidence-based
* **Focus:** Limited goals prevent distractions and enable prioritization

Without this clarity:

* Goals become unclear or contradictory
* Effort is misdirected and inefficient
* Performance evaluations feel subjective or unfair
* Employees feel disengaged or uncertain

## The WHAT + HOW Framework

At Checkout, goals are defined using two components: what you need to deliver, and how you will deliver it.

### WHAT: The Deliverable

* A specific outcome to achieve
* Defines "what success looks like"
* Must be measurable and clear
* Example: *Grow global payment volume by 20% in Q4*

### HOW: The Strategy / Approach

* The plan or method to achieve the goal
* Defines how the team or individual will deliver the outcome
* Must be actionable but does not replace the goal
* Example: *Launch in 3 new regions and optimise onboarding to reduce drop-off by 15% by end of Q4*

### Important Principles

* Always separate WHAT (outcome) from HOW (strategy)
* Use SMART principles: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound

## Goal Cascade Overview: From Company Level to You

Goals are cascaded from Company → Department → Team → Individual, ensuring line of sight and accountability.

| Level | Goal (What) | Approach (How) | Owner | Approver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Company | High-level business outcomes | Company-wide strategic initiatives | CEO Office | eTeam and CEO Office |
| Department | Dept. priorities supporting Company Goals | Department approaches | Functional Leader | Individual eTeam |
| Team | Team deliverables supporting Dept. Goals | Team strategies | Team Lead | Functional Leaders |
| Individual | Specific tasks contributing to Team Goals | How ICs achieve objectives | Employee | Managers |

**Note:** Individual and team goals should focus on outcomes you can directly influence. Avoid simply copying company-level goals — your goals must be ones where your actions can drive measurable results.

## How to Set Great Goals: The SMART Way

SMART is a simple framework to help you set goals that are clear, measurable, and achievable. Using SMART ensures everyone in the company knows what success looks like and can track progress. It can be used for Company, Department, Team, and Individual goals.

### Specific

**What it means:** Your goal must clearly define what you want to achieve. Avoid vague language.

**Questions to ask:**
* What exactly do we want to accomplish?
* Who is involved?
* Where will this happen?

**Good Example (Company Goal):** Increase global payment volume by 20% in Q4
**Poor Example:** Grow our business globally → no focus, no clarity
**Tip:** Always use action verbs and precise outcomes.

### Measurable

**What it means:** Include numbers or measurable indicators to track progress.

**Questions to ask:**
* How will we know when the goal is achieved?
* What metrics will we track?

**Good Example (Department Goal):** Launch 3 regional offices and hire 30 staff by 3 September
**Poor Example:** Hire staff quickly → subjective, no clear success metric
**Tip:** If you can't measure it, you can't manage it.

### Achievable

**What it means:** The goal should be ambitious but realistic given your resources and capacity.

**Questions to ask:**
* Is this goal realistic with current resources?
* Do we have the skills and tools to succeed?

**Good Example (Team Goal):** Onboard 1,000 merchants in new regions by Q4 → realistic with team size and resources
**Poor Example:** Onboard all merchants in the world by Q4 → unrealistic
**Tip:** Ambitious goals are good, but unrealistic goals demotivate.

### Relevant

**What it means:** The goal must support higher-level company objectives and be meaningful for the team or individual.

**Questions to ask:**
* Does this goal align with the company strategy?
* Does this goal matter for the team or individual role?

**Good Example (Individual Goal):** Close 50 new merchant accounts in Q4 → contributes directly to Team Goal → Department → Company
**Poor Example:** Create social media content → not linked to Sales or onboarding metrics
**Tip:** Always check the line-of-sight to higher-level goals.

### Time-bound

**What it means:** Set a deadline to create accountability and urgency.

**Questions to ask:**
* When should this goal be achieved?
* Are there interim milestones?

**Good Example (Company Goal):** Achieve $10M in new payment volume by 31 Dec
**Poor Example:** Increase payment volume eventually → no timeline, no urgency
**Tip:** Always set a clear end date or milestone.

## Step-by-Step: Building Company Goals

**Step 1: Identify Focus Area**
* Focus on measurable, high-impact outcomes
* Ensure alignment with company strategy and growth priorities

**Step 2: Define WHAT**
* Phrase as specific outcomes, not tasks
* Avoid mixing with HOW

**Step 3: Define HOW (Strategies)**
* List 2–3 high-level strategic approaches

**Step 4: Track Progress**
* Monitor progress against each WHAT by checking whether the HOW actions are being executed
* Use simple metrics or milestones defined in the WHAT and HOW to see if the goal is on track

**Tips for Leaders:**
* Keep language simple and consistent
* Limit the number of goals to avoid overload
* Ensure every goal aligns with the company's top priorities

### Example of Company Goal Setting

**High-Quality Company Goal Example:**
* WHAT: Increase global payment volume by 20% in Q4
* HOW: Launch in 3 new regions and optimise onboarding to reduce drop-off by 15% by end of Q4
* Progress Check: Are the 3 new regions launched on time? Are onboarding flows optimised? Are local marketing campaigns running as planned?
* Why it works: Clear, measurable, aligned with company priorities

**Poor-Quality Company Goal Example:**
* WHAT/HOW mix: Expand internationally and do marketing campaigns everywhere
* Why it's poor: Vague, mixes outcome with tactic, no metric → can't track success

## Step-by-Step: Building Department Goals

**Step 1: Align with Company Goals**
* Each department identifies contributions to Company Goals

**Step 2: Define WHAT**
* Specific departmental outcome

**Step 3: Define HOW**
* Actions or strategies to deliver the department goal

**Step 4: Track Progress**
* Monitor progress against the WHAT by checking whether the HOW actions are being executed
* Use simple, concrete indicators or milestones to see if the department goal is on track

**Tips for Leaders:**
* Focus on outcomes, not tasks
* Use SMART principles for clarity and measurability

### Example of Department Goal Setting

**High-Quality Department Goal Example:**
* Company Goal: Increase global payment volume by 20% in Q4
* Department Goal — WHAT: Launch 3 regional offices
* HOW: Register 3 regional offices, hire 15 staff total (5 per office), and set up 3 fully equipped offices by Oct 31
* Progress Check: Are the 3 offices operational on schedule? Are local staff hired? Is equipment and legal setup complete?
* Why it works: Specific and measurable outcome, clear strategies that support the goal, time-bound with measurable success indicators, directly aligned to Company Goal

**Poor-Quality Department Goal Example:**
* WHAT/HOW mix: Expand department presence and hire staff quickly
* Why it's poor: Vague outcome ("expand presence" → no clear definition of success); WHAT and HOW are mixed ("hire staff quickly" → tactic, not measurable outcome); no metrics or deadlines → cannot track progress; no clear link to Company Goal

## Step-by-Step: Building Team Goals

* Derived from Department Goals
* Assign clear ownership for each team goal

**Tips for Leaders:**
* Review team capacity before finalising goals
* Align with individual goals to ensure execution feasibility

### Example of Team Goal Setting

**High-Quality Team Goal Example:**
* Company Goal: Increase global payment volume by 20% in Q4
* Department Goal: Launch 3 regional offices
* Team Goal — WHAT: Onboard 1,000 merchants in new regions by Q4
* HOW: Conduct 10 webinars targeting merchants, run 5 partner campaigns, execute 20 local events
* Progress Check: Are 1,000 merchants onboarded by the deadline? Are the planned webinars, campaigns, and events executed on schedule? Does the work clearly support the Department Goal?
* Why it works: Specific and measurable outcome (1,000 merchants), clear strategies aligned with the Department Goal, time-bound (by Q4), direct contribution to Company Goal

**Poor-Quality Team Goal Example:**
* WHAT/HOW mix: Increase merchant engagement by doing webinars and campaigns
* Why it's poor: Vague outcome ("increase engagement" → no number, no definition of success); WHAT and HOW are mixed together; no measurable success indicators → can't track progress; no clear ownership

## Step-by-Step: Building Individual Goals

* Translate Team Goals into specific, measurable tasks for ICs
* Use SMART framework rigorously

**Tips for Leaders:**
* Ensure each IC understands how their work contributes to Team / Company goals
* Provide coaching on measuring progress
* Revisit and adjust goals if priorities change

### Example of Individual Goal Setting

**High-Quality Individual Goal Example:**
* Company Goal: Increase global payment volume by 20% in Q4
* Department Goal: Launch 3 regional offices
* Team Goal — WHAT: Onboard 1,000 merchants in new regions by Q4
* Individual Goal — WHAT: Close 50 new merchant accounts in Q4
* HOW: Conduct 20 cold calls per week, schedule 5 demo sessions per week, achieve $500k in new revenue
* Progress Check: Are 50 merchant accounts closed by the end of Q3? Are the planned cold calls and demos being executed consistently? Is the individual contribution clearly supporting the Team Goal?
* Why it's strong: Specific and measurable (50 accounts, $500k revenue), time-bound (Q4), clearly contributes to Team Goal, achievable and realistic

**Poor-Quality Individual Goal Example:**
* WHAT/HOW mix: Increase merchant sales by working hard and reaching out to clients
* Why it's poor: Vague ("increase sales" → no target number); subjective ("working hard" → cannot measure); no timeline or metric → no accountability; no clear link to Team Goal

## Governance & Approvals: Who Owns What (and When)

To ensure clarity, consistency, and fairness across Checkout, all goals follow a governance and approval process:

| Goal Level | Created by | Approved by |
|---|---|---|
| Company Goals | CEO Office (validated by Initiative Owners) | eTeam and CEO Office |
| Department Goals | Functional Leaders | Individual eTeam |
| Team Goals | Team Leaders | Functional Leaders |
| Individual Goals | Employees (with manager support) | Managers |

**Deadlines:** Each level has fixed submission and review windows. Departments and teams must meet their deadlines to enable individuals to set goals on time. Exact timelines will be communicated during the rollout.

## FAQs: Your Questions, Answered

### Goal Carryover

**What is copied over when carrying goals into a new cycle?**
Only the goal title and description are carried forward. Progress, status updates, comments, notes, and other historical information are not copied. Goals are intended to represent the priorities of each performance cycle, so Fabric does not support carrying an entire goal — including progress and updates — into a new cycle.

### Team Goals

**Do Department Leaders need to create new team goals for H2?**
Yes. Department Leaders should create their H2 team (PGT) goals by 15 July. The purpose of team goals is to create alignment across teams and provide a shared direction. Ultimately, every employee should also have their own individual goals aligned to these team goals.

**Can multiple teams have the same goal?**
Yes. Similar goals can exist across multiple teams (for example, UK, Americas and Europe). Each team should create its own team goal, with the appropriate owner, while ensuring alignment where relevant.

### Goal Ownership

**Is team ownership managed in Workday or Fabric?**
Team ownership continues to be managed in Workday. Goal ownership is managed directly within Fabric.

**What happens if someone changes teams?**
If an employee moves teams or departments, their goals remain with them. Goal ownership will need to be updated manually where appropriate.

### Shared Goals & Goal Linking

**How should shared goals be managed?**
There are two recommended approaches:
1. Create a team goal and allow team members to become co-owners where appropriate.
2. Alternatively, each individual can create their own goal and link it to the relevant team goal to demonstrate alignment.

**Can goals be linked across different teams?**
Yes. Goals can be linked across teams by managing ownership within Fabric, allowing broader organisational alignment where needed.

**How does progress work for shared team goals?**
For team goals, Fabric provides an option to calculate progress by taking the average of all linked individual goals. This ensures the overall team goal reflects collective progress.

### Metrics & Goal Progress

**How should I enter progress against percentage-based metrics?**
Progress should reflect the actual target, not the percentage of completion. Example: If your goal target is 70% and you achieve that target, record 70%, not 100%.

**Can goals measure performance over a period of time?**
Yes. Where appropriate, goals can be configured to track metrics such as an average performance over the previous six months.

**Can I amend status updates on my goals?**
No, it is not possible to edit or delete a status update once it has been submitted. If you need to correct a mistake, change the status, or provide new information, you will need to create a new status update that reflects the most accurate and current details for your goal.

### Integrations

**Can goals be linked to Looker or Jira?**
Fabric supports integrations with platforms such as Looker and Jira. It's important to note that integrations are most effective when goals have clearly defined, measurable metrics. Goals that are vague or not linked to quantifiable outcomes are less suitable for automated tracking.

### General Goal Guidelines

**What's the difference between Company, Department, Team, and Individual Goals?**
* Company Goals set the overall business direction.
* Department Goals define key priorities that help achieve Company Goals.
* Team Goals specify actionable objectives that support Department Goals.
* Individual Goals are measurable outcomes each person works on to drive Team and Company priorities.

Think of it as a clear line-of-sight from top to bottom, where every goal contributes to the bigger picture.

**How many goals should I set at each level?**
* Company Goals: 3 to 5 high-impact outcomes
* Departments: 2 to 4 priorities
* Teams: 2 to 3 objectives
* Individuals: 1 to 3 SMART goals

Fewer, well-defined goals drive focus and make alignment across the company much easier.

**How do I know if a goal is strong or weak?**
A strong goal is clear, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. It separates the outcome (WHAT) from the approach (HOW), and it links directly to higher-level objectives. A weak goal is vague, mixes outcome with tactics, lacks metrics, or has no timeline. Use the Strong vs Poor Examples in this Playbook as a reference.

**Can SMART be used for all goal levels?**
Yes. SMART applies at the Company, Department, Team, and Individual levels. The principles remain the same regardless of scale.

**Do I need to define HOW for every goal?**
Absolutely. The WHAT defines the outcome you want to achieve, while the HOW outlines the approach or strategy to get there. Separating the two keeps goals clear and actionable. At Team and Individual levels, the HOW often overlaps with initiatives or projects, but it should never be mixed with the WHAT.

**What if my goal changes mid-quarter or mid-year?**
Goals can be adjusted when business priorities shift. When that happens, update the WHAT and HOW, track progress, and communicate changes with your manager or team. Always ensure the revised goal still aligns with higher-level objectives.

**What if I'm unsure how my goal links to the Company Goal?**
Speak with your manager or department lead. Every goal should contribute, directly or indirectly, to the higher-level goal so that you can see the impact of your work on the company's success.

**How will my goals be used in performance evaluations?**
Goals provide clarity, alignment, and measurable outcomes. They form the basis for performance conversations, coaching, and fair evaluations. Remember, performance also includes the quality of work and behaviour; meeting goals alone is not enough.

**What support is available if I struggle to write a strong goal?**
This Playbook is your primary guide. Use SMART principles, review the Strong vs Poor Examples, and if needed, reach out to your manager, department lead, or People Partner for coaching. You are never expected to figure this out alone.

**Tip:** To ensure you set a strong goal, you can use the AI Goal Quality Tool feature in Fabric when adding your goals. This tool evaluates your goal's quality and provides suggestions and examples to help you improve it. Always aim to create high-quality, well-defined goals.

**I'm a Community Lead. How can I track and record my Community-Related work in Fabric?**
All community-specific goals must be explicitly labeled as "EXTRA" in the goal description. This identifies the work as a voluntary strategic contribution above the lead's core role. These goals are for recognition and tracking only. If a community goal is not met, it will have no negative impact on the individual's overall performance rating or compensation. Leads are encouraged to use wider "success brackets" (e.g., "Arab Community engagement increase of 5-10%") rather than binary numbers to provide a fair and achievable range. For more information, see Chapter 8 in the Community Handbook.
