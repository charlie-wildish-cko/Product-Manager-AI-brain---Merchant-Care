# Checkout.com — Business Context

> Sourced from checkout.com, February 2026. Refresh periodically as the business evolves.


## Company Overview

**Checkout.com** is a global Payment Service Provider (PSP) that enables businesses to accept, move, and optimise payments worldwide. Founded in 2012, it has grown into one of the world's largest independent payments platforms.

### Brand Positioning

> "Where the world checks out"

Checkout.com positions itself as the **invisible engine behind the digital economy** — infrastructure that works so seamlessly that neither consumers nor businesses need to think about it.

> "Customers get what they need, businesses get paid, and we get no recognition. Ideal. Because when payments just work, businesses grow, customers stay, and no one stops to think about why."

The brand is performance-obsessed:

> "High-performance payments move your business forward. It's why we obsess over every basis point."

### Scale (2025 figures)

- **$300 billion** in ecommerce payments processed in 2025
- **50+ countries** with local acquiring capabilities
- **150+ processing currencies**
- **19 offices worldwide**
- Millions of daily transactions for enterprise merchants globally


## Business Model

Checkout.com is a **B2B business** selling to merchants (businesses) of all sizes, with the heaviest focus on enterprise. Merchants integrate Checkout.com to:

- Accept payments from their customers (consumers)
- Move money globally
- Protect against fraud
- Optimise acceptance rates and conversion

**Revenue model**: Primarily transaction-based fees (per-payment processing fees), plus fees for value-added services.

### Customer Segments (B2B)

- **Enterprise**: Large multinational merchants (the primary focus)
- **Mid-market**: Growing businesses with significant payment volumes
- **SMB/Startup**: Smaller merchants getting started

### Notable Merchant Categories

Checkout.com serves businesses across many verticals, including:

- **Retail & ecommerce** (large online retailers)
- **Travel & hospitality** (booking platforms, ride-sharing)
- **Marketplaces** (buyers and sellers globally)
- **Digital goods** (streaming, gaming, SaaS)
- **Financial services** (fintech, crypto, BNPL)
- **Food & delivery** (online ordering platforms)


## Product Portfolio

Checkout.com organises its products into five pillars:

### 1. Connect — Payment Acceptance

*"Select your way to process payments online"*

Core payment processing infrastructure enabling merchants to accept payments from customers globally.

**Integration options**:

- **Hosted Payment Pages**: Checkout.com generates and hosts the payment page
- **Frames (SDK)**: Embedded card collection via iframe
- **API**: Full custom integration for maximum flexibility
- **Mobile SDKs**: Native iOS and Android integration

**Key capabilities**:

- Multi-currency acceptance
- Smart payment routing
- Real-time authorisation
- Tokenisation for repeat payments


### 2. Move — Money Movement

*"Simplify global money movement"*

Tools to manage how money flows globally — payins, payouts, and fund management across currencies and geographies.

**Includes**:

- Cross-border payouts
- Multi-currency accounts
- Settlement management
- Virtual card issuing
- B2B travel payments


### 3. Boost — AI-Powered Optimisation

*"Power performance with artificial intelligence"*

**Flagship product: Intelligent Acceptance**

> "Automatically optimize every payment with an AI engine designed to increase conversions and capture more revenue."

Key capabilities:

- **AI-driven payment routing**: Dynamically routes transactions for highest acceptance rate
- **Network tokens**: Replace card PANs with network tokens for higher auth rates
- **Smart retries**: Intelligently retry soft declines at optimal timing
- **Issuer insights**: Leverage data signals to improve authorisation

**Customer results** (from case studies):

- 4.15% uplift in acceptance rates
- 6% improvement in acceptance rate
- 200% growth in online experience bookings (5+ years)


### 4. Protect — Fraud, Risk & Compliance

*"Master fraud, risk and compliance"*

**Includes**:

- **Fraud Detection**: Real-time ML-based transaction screening
- **Authentication / 3DS**: 3D Secure implementation for SCA compliance
- **Identity Verification (KYC)**: Merchant onboarding compliance
- **Chargeback management**: Tools for dispute handling


### 5. Manage — Control & Operations

*"Control how money moves worldwide"*

**Merchant Dashboard**:

- Unified view of all payments, analytics, and KPIs
- Transaction search and investigation
- User and permissions management
- Settlement reports and reconciliation
- Configuration management


## Consumer Product (B2C — Personal)

Separate from B2B, Checkout.com offers a **consumer-facing product** under checkout.com/for-consumers:

- Consumers can **save card details once** and check out faster across all merchants using Checkout.com
- Protected by OTP authentication and encryption
- **Personal Dashboard** at personal-dashboard.checkout.com: view purchase history, manage saved cards
- Supported card types: Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Diners, Discover, Mada

This is relevant context for the 2027 B2C support expansion — this product already exists and may inform the support model for consumers.

## Strategic Direction (2026)

### Banking Evolution

Checkout.com is on a trajectory towards becoming a **bank** — moving beyond payment processing into storing funds and holding interest on balances. The timeline differs by segment:

- **B2C (2027)**: The consumer wallet launches as a banking product — Checkout.com will hold consumer funds and earn interest on consumer balances from day one
- **B2B (2028+)**: Merchant banking follows — storing merchant funds and offering interest/yield products to merchants

This represents a fundamental shift in the business model:

- **From**: Transaction fee-based PSP (revenue per payment processed)
- **To**: Financial institution holding deposits and earning yield on stored balances, alongside continued payment processing

**Implications for the support model:**
- A banking licence (FCA/PRA in UK, equivalent in other jurisdictions) brings materially different regulatory obligations than an e-money licence — including mandatory complaint handling timelines, FOS referral rights, Consumer Duty, and accessible channel requirements (typically including phone)
- New query categories will emerge: B2C from 2027 (savings, interest, balance disputes); B2B from 2028 (merchant balance management, yield queries, treasury-style questions)
- The competitive set broadens: Checkout will compete with Monzo, Revolut, and Starling for consumer banking relationships (2027) and Revolut Business, Starling Business, and Tide for merchant banking (2028+)
- Support becomes a regulated function, not just a commercial one — standards, response times, and escalation paths will have compliance obligations attached

> B2B support implications (2028+): `care-product-model.md` → B2B Banking Evolution.

## Customer Support Implications

### P&L Context for Support

Customer Support sits on the **Loss (L)** side of the Product department P&L. It is not revenue-generating, but it is revenue-protecting. Across the Product org, each team is required to justify investment through a P&L lens — for Care/Support, the goal is to demonstrate that unit costs (cost per contact, support cost per $1M processed) decrease over time even as transaction volume and merchant count grow.

Key framing:
- The right measure is **unit cost declining**, not absolute cost — absolute spend may grow alongside the business
- Investment in AI deflection, contact reduction (Reflex), agent tooling, and self-service all have direct cost trajectories
- Support quality is a **churn-protection mechanism**: this argument is strongest for SMB B2B and B2C (low switching costs, no account management layer) and is less direct for enterprise today (long contracts, dedicated AMs buffer against support-driven churn)

> Full P&L reporting guidance and metric framework: `01-knowledge-base/metrics/kpi-definitions.md` → P&L Reporting section


### Why Merchants Contact Support

Understanding the business context helps anticipate support topics:

**Integration complexity**: Checkout.com offers many integration paths (API, SDK, hosted pages, mobile) → integration questions are common

**Payment method breadth**: 50+ payment methods across 50+ countries → configuration and troubleshooting across many combinations

**Performance optimisation**: Merchants care deeply about acceptance rates → questions about declined payments, routing, and AI features

**Settlement complexity**: Multi-currency, multi-market → reconciliation and settlement questions

**Compliance requirements**: PCI, SCA/3DS, PSD3 across markets → compliance-related questions

**AI features**: Intelligent Acceptance is complex → questions about how it works, why decisions were made

### Support for Enterprise Merchants

Checkout.com's core customer is enterprise — these merchants:

- Have high transaction volumes (small issues = large financial impact)
- Often have dedicated technical teams
- Expect high SLA adherence
- May have dedicated account managers
- Require detailed audit trails and reporting

### 2027 B2C Expansion Context

The **personal.checkout.com** consumer product already exists (card saving, OTP checkout). As this scales and mobile/phone support is added, consumer queries will differ significantly:

- Simpler questions (not API integration, but "why did my payment fail?")
- Higher volume, lower average complexity
- Different tone (consumer-friendly vs. developer-friendly)
- New regulatory considerations (consumer protection, GDPR)
- Potentially different SLA expectations
