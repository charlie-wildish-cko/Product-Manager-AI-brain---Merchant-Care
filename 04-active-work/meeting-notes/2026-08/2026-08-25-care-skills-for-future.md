## Context

Session on what the Care agent skill set needs to look like as AI automation removes L1 work, and on the performance-management gap that blocks getting there.

## Key Points

**Training and knowledge engagement is failing**
- Responsible AI / Glean smart-use sessions: 13 of 40 invited attended (detail section records 12 of 41 in the morning session, 1 in the afternoon).
- Agents are not completing Robert Braam's mandatory weekly knowledge quizzes. No repercussions exist for non-completion.
- Quiz scores average 50-60% on basic multiple-choice questions: a ~40% deficit on foundational payments knowledge.
- Robert described a historic pattern of agents prioritising preferred tasks over required development.

**Headcount and automation**
- This year's 40% FTE reduction target delivered 4-5 FTE. Working assumption for next year is a 50% reduction from AI automation.
- L1 roles absorb the automation impact. L2 becomes more critical, with direction of travel toward a support engineer model for bespoke merchant interactions.
- Automation impact starts to bite in roughly 6 months (Feb/Mar). Major volume shifts expected from 2028.
- SMB and enterprise contact volumes are modelled roughly flat, which weakens the case for holding current agent count in those roles.
- Rehousing options for displaced agents: managing AI solutions, QA review work, secondments. Open risk raised: moving underperformers transfers the problem rather than solving it.

**Performance management gap**
- Robert estimates ~50% of the current team is underperforming on basic tasks.
- There is no agent performance scorecard, no clear KPIs, and no consistent performance management in Merchant Care.
- An AI judge rolls out end of August to produce quality-breach data. Discussion covered resetting scorecards, defining metrics precisely, and pushing visibility into monthly business reviews with Tim.
- Debated whether a dedicated quality team plus root-cause reporting is needed for leadership to read quality metrics correctly.
- Karolina's document on Merchant Care performance was reviewed: weekly targets judged unrealistic, and the data showed David and others significantly overstated time spent and number of quality checks completed per week.

**Decisions**
- Adopt the skills framework named in the transcript as "Kzia" as the standard model for the future Care agent skill set. Gemini transcription is unreliable here: confirm the actual framework name before reuse.
- Use AI-driven quality monitoring plus current data to rank agents, forcing an upskill-or-manage-out choice.
- Use the 40-50% AI-driven reduction as the lever to push Jenny on underperformance by end of year or early next year.

## Insights

- The 50% FTE reduction assumption is the political lever being used to force a performance reset, not just a cost forecast.
- Care ops has no scorecard, so the AI judge (live end Aug 2026) becomes the first objective quality dataset and effectively defines the performance baseline.
- Two reusable evidence numbers for the agent-experience and Agent Consultant investment case: ~50% of the team underperforming, 50-60% average quiz scores.
- Volume nuance that cuts against a simple "automation absorbs growth" story: SMB and enterprise are flat and the big shifts only arrive from 2028, so near-term reduction is automation-driven, not demand-driven.
- Rehousing displaced L1 agents into QA and AI-solution management only works if performance is addressed first: otherwise the AI judge data will simply relocate the same problem.
