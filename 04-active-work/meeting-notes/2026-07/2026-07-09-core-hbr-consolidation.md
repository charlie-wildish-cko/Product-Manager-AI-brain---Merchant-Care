## Context

Working session to consolidate Charlie's (Care) and Gilles's (Risk & Compliance) separate business-review documents into one file for an HBR-style review. "Core" chosen as the framing word (indispensable — take it away and the business dies); consolidated doc named Core HBR.

## Key Points

**System consolidation**
- Reducing case management systems for Consumer: expect to be challenged on how this is rationalized. Argument: solving Consumer first paves the way for the rest, though technically it may create more systems short-term. Accept the initial launch will be rough and under-resourced.
- Salesforce volume: Gilles is likely the single biggest case producer in Salesforce — potentially ~10,000 cases/month including sanction screening. Wants to move off Salesforce eventually but warns against fragmenting into three disparate systems (the current trajectory).
- Long-term stance: Care/Risk/Compliance should converge onto one platform. Near-term, Care goes first because the Zendesk renewal timeline is driving the decision. Deliberately avoiding a deep sequencing debate now. Candidate for a joint top-3 executive-summary item: "we can't merge systems now, but we know it's the problem, here are the volumes, here's what we can do now."

**Proactive planning**
- Checkout historically only acts when "the s*** hits the fan." Consensus: scope large problems ~12 months in advance.
- Some current investments (e.g. Gilles's TM/sanctions automation) aren't justified by today's volumes but are prep for future scale. Metrics should reflect projections, not just today's numbers. Risk/Compliance projections are currently less accurate than Care's and need to improve.

**Operational intake friction**
- Care has a standard intake/change-logging process (ops team does much of the testing for new flows), so BAU change requests aren't a problem.
- Risk/Compliance suffers from "out of bounds" initiatives — large, poorly-scoped items landing without warning. Named examples: Project Ray (crypto wallet), "Blue," and EMI projects. Assumed company-supported but don't follow normal scoping channels — disruptive to holistic roadmap planning. Contrast: Consumer was flagged a year ahead (ambitious but not a surprise); Ray was not properly scoped. Oliver was pinging Charlie about Ray the same day.

**Consumer regulatory/compliance burden**
- Consumer introduces substantially more complex requirements than the current B2B model, pressuring both product and operations, who lack deep expertise.
- FCA vulnerable-customer obligations — broad definition, from elderly needing help to someone being coerced/scammed while making the transaction themselves (owned with Fabio). Hard on both detection and human handling.
- Strict regulatory reporting — e.g. complaint SLAs must be reported to the FCA across every system the complaint touched.

## Decisions

- Consolidate the two docs into one using Google Doc tabs (exec summary, highlights, lowlights, North Star metrics, H2 priorities, appendix).
- Consolidated doc named Core HBR.
- Use Claude to merge/mashup the distinct sections into cohesive key messages.
- Remove the OKR section.
- North Star metrics stay split by domain (Care vs Risk & Compliance) but themes should read as one; H2 priorities stay separate. Merge exec summary, highlights, lowlights, and help-needed.

## Insights

- Care–Risk–Compliance system convergence is emerging as a shared strategic theme, triggered by the Zendesk renewal (Care first) and Consumer expansion. Watch the tension: consolidate onto one platform vs the current drift toward three separate systems. Salesforce (~10k cases/month in Risk) is the incumbent Risk wants off.
- Consumer is the forcing function for both system rationalization and a step-change in regulatory complexity (FCA vulnerable customers, complaint SLA reporting) — consistent with the B2C wallet 2027 / Consumer Duty context. Product and Ops both lack the expertise today.
- Governance gap: poorly-scoped, out-of-bounds initiatives (Project Ray crypto wallet, Blue, EMI) landing without advance warning are the biggest disruptor to roadmap planning. Proposed fix — mandatory 12-month advance scoping for large initiatives — is a candidate leadership ask for the HBR.
- "Core HBR" and the "indispensable/core" framing is the narrative device for this review. Metrics-should-reflect-projections is a shared positioning point, justifying prep investments that today's volumes don't.
