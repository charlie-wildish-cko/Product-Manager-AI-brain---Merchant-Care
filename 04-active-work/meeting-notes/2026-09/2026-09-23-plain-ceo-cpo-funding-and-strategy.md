# Plain CEO and CPO: funding, strategy and fit

**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Attendees:** Charlie Wildish, Plain CEO, Plain CPO
**Source:** Charlie's notes from the call

## Context

Vendor viability session for the Zendesk replacement decision, covering scorecard row 6.9 (vendor continuity through 2030). Also covered Plain's target market, differentiators, and why prospects decline.

## Key Points

**Target customer**
- Today: technically capable businesses that build and control their own support experience and integrate their own systems.
- Longer term: less technical markets. Plain's view is that solving for technical customers first makes the move to simpler use cases easier, since the product is not limited by what it can do.

**Differentiators versus competitors**
- API-first and config as code are the main differentiators.
- Support is shifting from handling one ticket at a time to AI agents working through batches of work. Plain aims to provide the orchestration for that: agents operate wherever they need to get information and solve the work, customers plug in their own agents, and workflows run in batches.
- Intelligence and knowledge: the constraint is what knowledge is available about a ticket or the product, and building that into the support flow.

**Funding**
- Capitalised to the end of 2028.
- Plans to raise a Series B in January 2027 and is confident about it.
- Willing to put Checkout in touch with investors for due diligence.

**Why prospects do not sign**
- A required feature is missing and cannot be delivered within the prospect's timeline.
- The value of switching is judged lower than staying with a more mature vendor that has more features.

## Decisions

- Scorecard row 6.9 scored at 1 (vendor's own claim, unverified). Rises to 2 once investor due diligence confirms the runway and Series B position.

## Insights

- Plain's stated strategy matches Checkout's target architecture: API-first, config as code, own agents plugged in, orchestration of batched work by AI agents. The two differentiators Plain names are the two criteria where Plain leads the scorecard most clearly.
- Runway to end 2028 covers the migration and roughly 18 months after it, but not the 2030 continuity requirement on its own. The Series B is what closes that gap, so investor diligence is a precondition for contract signature.
- Neither decline reason applies to Checkout. Our three required gaps were committed in writing with dates, and the switching case rests on Zendesk's April 2026 AI decision and the cost of its gaps.
