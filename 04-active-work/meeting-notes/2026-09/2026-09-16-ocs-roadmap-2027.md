## Context

Shaping the 2027 OCS roadmap: platform consolidation sequencing, automation targets, and the accountability gap on contact drivers.

## Key Points

**Platform consolidation is priority one**

- Charlie: one platform, to avoid dual-running and duplicated configuration. Configuration is the heavy lift; the integration itself is just a workspace ID change in code, not a re-integration.
- Migration phasing: **consumer support first, Jan-Feb-Mar milestone, driven by the Ray milestone.** Merchant support in parallel, pacing TBD. Email is the hardest channel because the email server must plug directly into the new system. Merchant support experience to be segmented separately for internal tracking and traceability.
- Migration decision: **model the new team structure first, then migrate the support model and traffic onto it.** Do not copy legacy configuration across.
- Lessons from the Zendesk migration: easier this time because a strong baseline for desired structure already exists. Avoid a big bang: last time was 4 phases, this time 2-3, be more aggressive about cutting data down, and minimise dual-system running, which is worse than the migration itself.
- Charlie flagged a concern that a Plain route may not come with much implementation help, then noted the expectation that Plain would in fact offer build/design support and prioritisation sessions.

**Automation**

- Fin automation target 60% for H1 or H2, described by Charlie as arbitrary and needing firmer metrics based on query complexity and content coverage.
- For work Fin cannot resolve, create a **back-office automation bucket** and reuse existing services (transcribed as "Clean and ORCP", likely Plain and ORCP: verify).
- **Fin data bug resolved in explanation:** Intercom and Looker Fin numbers did not reconcile because direct escalations, mostly Remitly, were counted as Fin failures. Remove them and the resolution rate stabilises to its previous level. A fix is underway. August figure: roughly 5,000 tickets touched at ~48% resolution. Use the Intercom metrics; the Looker view should already be updated.

**Root cause fixes and accountability**

- Unresolved triage loops to be addressed by making root-cause investigation and Jira ticketing mandatory for product teams, fed by a new data service.
- Explore "Agent How" (transcription uncertain), an engineering AI agent, to auto-fix simple issues such as data errors and labelling. Effectiveness depends on complexity. Sidekick could drive agents for actions like refund reversals.
- **Accountability gap:** Care produces the data and identifies what needs fixing but does not own delivery timelines. Product pillars do, and they have no financial incentive to fix contact drivers because **support cost sits on Milan's P&L**. That makes APM and reconciliation fixes a constant fight. Charlie: products face changing requirements and lack maintenance capacity to fix bugs quickly.
- Product onboarding as a system: systematise launches so impact assessment, taxonomy alignment and content generation happen upfront, tying product changes into the resolution architecture and reducing basic support volume over time.
- Sequencing judgement: whether 60% automation stays reachable once easy issues are eliminated. Answer is that new problems keep arriving. Root-cause fixes have the highest theoretical value, but the group agreed **resolution is the higher-impact short-term focus** because influencing product engineering code is complex and resisted.

**Segmentation and cost**

- Split goals across merchant, consumer and SMB (SMB possibly merged with merchant), because query mixes differ and blending them hides the signal.
- AI resolution should prioritise high-volume queries with no internal dependencies. Dependency-heavy complex queries need separate handling plus a content strategy.
- Harlie: ticket handling cost rises with ticket duration, number of handoffs, and the seniority tier involved, with engineering the most expensive. Charlie: reduce L1 escalations and expand automation to control it. Scaling into Mexico increases the need for fast response to volume spikes.
- Analytics: shift from backward-looking reporting to predictive/proactive monitoring, prompted by the Remitly influx causing operational stress. Harlie and Tim to research alerting.

## Decisions

- Model the new team structure first, then migrate support model and traffic onto it rather than replicating legacy structure.
- Resolution over root-cause as the short-term priority.

## Insights

- The apparent Fin resolution drop was a data artefact, not a performance regression: Remitly direct escalations were mislabelled as Fin failures. Do not cite the dip in any business case.
- August baseline: ~5,000 tickets touched by Fin at ~48% resolution. The 60% automation target is still unvalidated with no agreed measurement method, and defining that methodology is a prerequisite for using it as a 2027 goal.
- Consumer support migration is pinned to Ray's Jan-Mar 2027 milestone, and that sequencing is what makes the platform decision urgent.
- Technical migration cost is low (workspace ID change). Configuration and flow redesign is where the effort sits, so vendor implementation support is a real selection criterion.
- The structural problem worth restating to leadership: product pillars carry no P&L consequence for the support cost they create, because it lands on Milan's P&L. Everything in the root-cause and prevention agenda is blocked on that incentive gap.
