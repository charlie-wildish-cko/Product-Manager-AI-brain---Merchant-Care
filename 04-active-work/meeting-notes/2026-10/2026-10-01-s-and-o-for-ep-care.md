## Context

Intro between Charlie and Komal after PMO merged into the Office of the CEO as Strategy & Operations (S&O). Purpose: agree how S&O supports Care's 2027 operations platform migration.

## Key Points

**S&O model**
- PMO merged with the corporate transformation team and moved into the Office of the CEO. Reason: initiatives are increasingly cross-functional and extend beyond product (Treasury, crypto merchants).
- S&O embeds in programs like a Treasury or Legal partner. Remit: portfolio reporting, blockers, escalation, C-level visibility, planning, tooling. It does not replace PM work.
- Komal covers Issuing and OCS (Care sits in OCS), plus Emerging Products and Consumer. A dedicated S&O person for OCS starts in December. Platforms already has one.

**Care scope and strategy**
- Charlie owns the product and technology side of Care and wants to rebrand "merchant care" to "care" (now includes consumer). His focus is AI and automation, not human-agent tooling.
- About 70% of contact volume is the payment lifecycle: payment, decline or success, proof for the customer, money arriving in the bank.
- Strategy has two arms: resolve via automation and AI, and feed experience data back to product teams. The biggest blocker is getting product teams to prioritise root causes. Example: no merchant-facing way to track a payment end to end through to funds received.
- Charlie frames AI as a bypass for answers that should already be available. The goal is "no support needed". Years of accumulated support debt compound as SMB and Platforms expand.

**2027 "operations platform" roadmap**
1. Scalable architecture that identifies segment (merchant, SMB, platform, consumer) instantly and gives the right automation-first support.
2. Move off Zendesk (implemented 2024, Charlie's second migration) to Plain, a startup on a "build partner" model. Checkout is a tiny tier in Zendesk's customer base and aims to be a marquee Plain customer. The risk is acknowledged.
3. Build the Ray consumer support flow.
4. Migrate merchant support.
- Other 2027 goals: higher share of support via AI, better agent tooling, cross-team flow optimisation.

**Ops fragmentation**
- Other ops teams still use Gmail inboxes, with no structured hand-offs, routing logic, or shared customer history. Merchants get bounced between teams.
- Enterprise tolerates this (account manager discounts). SMB and end users churn easily, so churn risk is much higher.

**Product launch impact**
- Most contacts come from card processing and APMs. Some APMs run at hundreds of contacts per million transactions against about 10 for other products. APM re-platforming is planned next year; it is unclear whether support impact drives it.

**MCAP**
- The ops platform is cross-functional so goes through MCAP. Benefit: committed capacity from other teams and delays surfaced to leadership (for example 3 months ahead).

**Scope and sequencing**
- Biggest open question: which ops teams are in migration scope. Needed to sequence, possibly in phases.
- Plan: a working group with a representative per ops team (workflows, tooling, tasks), via Oliver Westlake-Simm's process team. Ops is historically political and territorial and needs strong decision-making.
- Charlie is finishing the Zendesk-exit recommendation first. The work stream starts in about 2 weeks, with a decision milestone in November. Roadmap publication dates (floated in product, not yet business-wide) set the timeline.

## Insights

- S&O is a new escalation and governance lever, and MCAP is the route to force cross-team commitment.
- Undefined ops scope is the main risk to the 2027 migration plan.
- The Plain pitch is a build-partner relationship, which is also a concentration risk.
- Decision: establish the operations platform working group, facilitated by S&O.
