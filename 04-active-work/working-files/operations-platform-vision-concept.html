One operations platform. One record of work.

The problem

Our operations tooling is built around products, not customers. Each product team ships its own tools, queues and processes, so people absorb the fragmentation. Customers repeat themselves, are bounced between teams, hear conflicting messages, cannot see where their request is, and wait on slow decisions. Account managers mask this for white-glove customers. At Platforms, Consumer and SMB scale it gets worse.

What "poor" means
They repeat themselves
Periodic reviews ask for data the merchant has already provided, because it sits in another system.
They are bounced between teams
CSAT drops 20% when work moves from Care to another Ops team.
They hear conflicting messages
Care messaged a merchant about goodwill after commercial had already agreed a fee reduction.
They cannot see where it is
Once work is with another team it is gone from Care's view. Tracking it means searching an email inbox.
They wait on slow decisions
One reviewer approves every onboarding case, so every merchant waits on one person.
They switch between tools
About 5 tools on one Care L1 ticket. A periodic review moves through CAT, Genesis, the RA Tool and Looker just to decide if it is needed.
They create and copy cases by hand
Cases are created by hand or by email-to-case and assigned by hand. The configuration team copies Salesforce cases into CAT.
They redo checks
A risk alert already reviewed by another team is checked again, because nothing links the earlier outcome.
They cannot find the answer
Knowledge sits in 8 places across 21 sub-functions, and search does not put guidance in the workflow.
They cannot see what other teams did
Teams do not share customer data or communications history, so account managers fill the gap.
Cost does not fall with scale
Care contacts grow up to 36x by 2030, with about 5 tools and manual reassignment on each ticket. The yearly Ops headcount ask cannot be challenged, because work is not measured.
It cannot measure what to fix
There is no link between a piece of work and the tool used to do it, so efficiency across Operations cannot be measured.
Regulatory reporting is manual
Complaints reports take hours to collate end to end, and SLAs cannot be reported consistently across teams.
Risk is exposed by delay
A review flagged in May 2024 was still pending while the merchant kept processing.
Quality is not checked
Manual QA covers under 1% of Care volume.
For customers

For Checkout

No transparent reporting, with fine and breach exposure.
Today in practice (illustrative)

Six examples built from the Ops review (2026-10-07), the Mauritius site visit and Care's cross-team dependencies. In each, no step shares a record.

Customer-initiated
1The merchant asks Care about a dispute.
2Care cannot see or action it, so tells the merchant to email the Disputes team. To the merchant, Care and Disputes are both just Checkout.
3The merchant emails the Disputes inbox. From here, Care cannot see the status.
4The merchant waits, then follows up with Care, not the Disputes team, because they do not know the difference.
5Care emails Disputes for status. Disputes replies by email, outside the Dashboard, so the merchant's Care request still looks unanswered.

Work with another team is "gone" from Care's view, and the merchant cannot tell which team to ask.

Customer-initiated
1The merchant asks Care where their payout is.
2Care cannot see the payout status, so raises a request to Treasury.
3Treasury works it in its own queue and tools. Care has no view of progress.
4The merchant chases Care for an update.
5Care chases Treasury. The answer comes back later and Care relays it.

Care depends on Treasury to confirm payouts, and neither Care nor the merchant can trace the status.

Customer-initiated, via commercial
1The merchant applies, and commercial opens the case in Salesforce.
2Documents move from Salesforce to Drive.
3The team tracks progress on a spreadsheet and runs MCC checks by hand.
4Approvals are requested over Gmail, and one reviewer approves every case.

Four tools and no shared record, and every case waits on one person.

Checkout-initiated: we asked first
1A transaction monitoring alert opens a case in Salesforce.
2The analyst works the case in Retool. Salesforce does not see the work until the case closes.
3The analyst asks the merchant for information.
4When the merchant replies, the case returns to a queue and is reassigned by hand.
5The case syncs back to Salesforce only when it closes.

Salesforce holds the case but not the work. The record is only complete at close.

Checkout-initiated: we asked first
1The periodic reviews team emails the merchant, asking for sensitive information.
2The merchant cannot tell if it is legitimate, so asks their account manager.
3The account manager cannot see the request, so asks the reviews team.
4The reviews team confirms it, and the account manager tells the merchant it is genuine.
5The merchant sends the sensitive information by email.

Nothing in the merchant's Dashboard or Care shows the request, and there is no company-approved way to ask for sensitive information or scan what comes back.

Customer-initiated
1The merchant emails Care about a fee, and Care opens a Zendesk ticket.
2Care asks the account manager and commercial on Slack, outside any case, whether goodwill or a fee reduction applies.
3The account manager has already agreed a fee reduction with the merchant by email. Care cannot see it.
4Care messages the merchant again about remediation. The merchant asks why they are being messaged.
5The change starts as a Salesforce case. The Configuration team copies it into CAT by hand.
6If this became a formal complaint, Care would have to build the complaint record by hand from Zendesk, email, Slack and Salesforce. It takes hours.

Every handoff is a message, not a record. The merchant, the account manager and Care each absorb the gap.

The vision: what good looks like
For customers
Ask once, hear one voice, see where it is.
  • They never repeat themselves, whoever they deal with.
  • They are never bounced between teams.
  • They hear one consistent message, and know a request from us is genuine.
  • They can see where their request is, and decisions do not wait on one person.
For human agents
Work in one place, with the context to decide.
  • They see the customer's history and what other teams did.
  • They never create, copy or chase cases by hand.
  • They do not redo checks another team has done.
  • Guidance is in the workflow.
For Checkout
Operations that scale with volume, not headcount, and can prove what happened.
  • Cost per contact falls as volume grows.
  • Every piece of work is measured, so we know what to fix.
  • Regulatory reports come from the record, not hours of stitching.
  • Risk decisions land on time, and quality is checked on all work.
The approach

Every piece of work, whether a customer comes to us or we go to them first, is resolved by the cheapest capable resolver: self-serve, then the customer agent, then a human for customer-initiated work, and an agent or a human by task type and risk for Checkout-initiated work. Support, Risk and Compliance work on one case system, and Care gives the customer one place to ask and see status.

Proposed sequence and how each phase is measured (timing TBC against the 2026 deliverables)

Baseline
Q4 2026: Design
Work types from SOPs. Vision and system design agreed by Support, Risk and Compliance.
  • Work types inventoried: % of SOP work types
  • Baselines captured: cost per case, handle time, cycle time, CSAT by team
  • Sponsor named, XFN group released
Build
Dec 2026: Ray friends and family
Care handles consumer support on Plain. First proof point.
  • Work types live vs plan
  • Cases created through structured intake, not email: %
  • Actions in tools linked to a case: % (audit completeness, handle time measurable)
  • Fin involvement rate and AI resolution rate
  • Work mix by work type: automated, AI-assisted, human only
Migrate
H1 2027: Acquiring support
Care handles Acquiring support on Plain. Ray external launch date unconfirmed.
  • Care volume on the platform vs legacy: %
  • Tools per ticket (about 5 today) and tool actions linked to a case: %
  • QA coverage (under 1% today, target 100%)
  • Agents working in the platform: % of active agents
  • Care work mix vs baseline (16-18% automated today)
Migrate and retire
From H2 2027: Other teams
Risk and Compliance teams move in the agreed sequence. Shared tools and AI agents: TBC.
  • Teams on the platform: n of total
  • Case systems and email inboxes: to one case system. Retool apps retired (20+ in Care alone)
  • CSAT drop on cross-team handoff (20% today)
  • Time to compile a complaints report (hours today)
Migration guardrails, checked at every cutover: Merchant CSAT (reported by segment), SLA attainment, backlog age and handle time stay at or better than the team's Q4 baseline. The next team moves only when the previous team's guardrails hold. Targets for every measure above are set from the Q4 baselines.
