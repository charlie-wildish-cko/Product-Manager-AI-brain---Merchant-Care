# Stakeholder Updates Workflow

> This workflow guides you through creating effective, regular updates for stakeholders on projects and initiatives.

## Why Stakeholder Updates Matter

- **Maintain alignment**: Keep everyone informed on progress and direction
- **Build trust**: Transparency builds confidence in your leadership
- **Enable decisions**: Surface blockers and decisions needed
- **Manage expectations**: Set realistic timelines and scope
- **Celebrate progress**: Recognize wins and team contributions
- **Reduce interruptions**: Proactive updates reduce ad-hoc status requests


## Step 1: Define Your Audience

### Identify Stakeholders

**Who needs updates?**
- Product leadership (VP, Directors)
- Engineering teams (leads and individual contributors)
- Design and UX team
- Support team
- Sales/Account Management
- Operations teams
- External partners (if applicable)

### Segment by Information Needs

**Different audiences need different details**:

**Executives**:
- High-level progress
- Business impact and metrics
- Strategic alignment
- Decisions and blockers requiring leadership
- Budget and resource implications

**Engineering Teams**:
- Technical progress and milestones
- Blockers and dependencies
- Scope changes or new requirements
- Design decisions affecting implementation

**Support/Operations**:
- Customer-facing changes
- Training needs
- Process changes
- Timeline for merchant communication

**Cross-functional Partners**:
- How this affects their work
- What you need from them
- Shared milestones
- Integration points


## Step 2: Determine Cadence

### Regular Updates

**Weekly**:
- **Best for**: Active projects in execution phase
- **Format**: Brief written update (5 min read)
- **Contents**: Progress, upcoming work, immediate blockers
- **Audience**: Core project team and direct stakeholders

**Bi-weekly**:
- **Best for**: Longer-term initiatives with steady progress
- **Format**: Written update with optional sync meeting
- **Contents**: Sprint summaries, metrics, medium-term plans

**Monthly**:
- **Best for**: Strategic initiatives, multiple projects, portfolio view
- **Format**: Comprehensive written report + presentation
- **Contents**: Trends, achievements, roadmap changes, resource needs
- **Audience**: Broader stakeholder group, leadership

**Quarterly**:
- **Best for**: Business reviews, strategic planning
- **Format**: Formal presentation with detailed report
- **Contents**: Quarter achievements, next quarter plans, budget review

### Event-Driven Updates

**When to send ad-hoc updates**:
- Major milestone reached
- Significant blocker or delay
- Critical decision needed
- Scope or timeline change
- Risk materialized
- Incident or crisis
- User feedback requiring attention


## Step 3: Gather Information

### Collect Data

**Before writing, gather**:

**Quantitative Data**:
- [ ] Metrics and KPIs (from dashboards)
- [ ] Ticket volumes (if relevant)
- [ ] Usage statistics (if launched)
- [ ] Timeline status (on track, behind, ahead)
- [ ] Resource utilization
- [ ] Budget spend

**Qualitative Information**:
- [ ] Team progress reports
- [ ] Blocker details
- [ ] Customer feedback
- [ ] Design decisions made
- [ ] Technical learnings
- [ ] Stakeholder concerns raised

**Project Status**:
- [ ] Completed tasks/milestones
- [ ] In-progress work
- [ ] Upcoming priorities
- [ ] Risks and issues
- [ ] Decisions needed

### Review Previous Update

- What did you promise to do?
- Did you do it?
- What follow-ups are due?
- What questions were asked that need answering?


## Step 4: Structure Your Update

Use template: `03-templates/stakeholder-update-template.md`

### Essential Components

#### 1. TL;DR (Top 3 Things)
Lead with what matters most:
- Most important achievement
- Biggest risk/blocker
- Key upcoming milestone

**Why**: Busy people may only read this section

#### 2. Progress
What got done since last update:
- Completed milestones
- Shipped features
- Decisions made
- Problems solved

**Be specific**:
- ❌ "Made good progress on API"
- ✅ "Completed API authentication flow; 3 of 5 endpoints now functional"

#### 3. Metrics
Show progress with numbers:
- Project-specific KPIs
- Support metrics (if relevant)
- Usage data (if launched)
- Business impact

**Use comparisons**:
- Current vs. previous period
- Actual vs. target
- Trend direction (↑↓)

#### 4. Upcoming Work
What's next:
- Priorities for next period
- Key milestones coming up
- Timeline for deliverables

**Set expectations**:
- What stakeholders will see
- When they'll see it
- What input you need from them

#### 5. Blockers & Risks
Surface problems clearly:
- What's blocking progress
- What risks are concerning you
- What decisions are needed

**For each blocker**:
- What's blocked
- Impact if not resolved
- Who needs to act
- Deadline for action

#### 6. Help Needed
Be specific about asks:
- What you need
- From whom
- By when
- Why it's important

**Make it easy to help**:
- Specific action, not vague request
- Clear deadline
- Context on why it matters


## Step 5: Write Clear, Actionable Content

### Use Concise Language

**Keep it brief**:
- Short paragraphs (3-4 sentences max)
- Bullet points for lists
- Tables for comparisons
- Subheadings for navigation

**Avoid**:
- Unnecessary jargon
- Long explanations
- Redundant information
- Vague statements

### Highlight What's Important

**Use visual cues**:
- **Bold** for key points
- 🔴 Red emoji for blockers
- 🟢 Green for on-track
- 🟡 Yellow for at-risk
- ✅ Checkmark for completed
- ⏳ Clock for upcoming

### Include Links

**Link to**:
- Full PRD or spec
- Dashboards and metrics
- Design files
- Meeting notes
- Related tickets
- Project board

**Why**: Makes it easy for stakeholders to dive deeper


## Step 6: Adapt for Audience

### Executive Summary Version

**For leadership, emphasize**:
- Business impact and ROI
- Strategic alignment
- Risk and mitigation
- Resource implications
- High-level timeline

**Keep brief**: 2-3 paragraphs max

### Technical Deep Dive Version

**For engineering teams, include**:
- Technical milestones achieved
- Architecture decisions
- Performance metrics
- Technical debt considerations
- Engineering challenges and solutions

### Operations Version

**For support/ops teams, focus on**:
- Customer-facing changes
- Training requirements
- Process updates
- Timeline for rollout
- What to tell customers


## Step 7: Review & Edit

### Self-Review Checklist

- [ ] **Clarity**: Can someone unfamiliar with the project understand this?
- [ ] **Accuracy**: Are all facts and numbers correct?
- [ ] **Completeness**: Did I address everything I promised last time?
- [ ] **Actionability**: Are asks and next steps clear?
- [ ] **Tone**: Is it professional, clear, and appropriately urgent?
- [ ] **Length**: Is it concise enough for people to actually read?
- [ ] **Links**: Do all links work?

### Common Improvements

**Replace vague terms**:
- "Soon" → "By end of Q2"
- "Many" → "67% of merchants"
- "We're making progress" → "Completed 4 of 7 milestones"

**Be honest about problems**:
- Don't sugarcoat delays or challenges
- Explain what you're doing about it
- Set realistic expectations

**Add context**:
- Why does this matter?
- How does it connect to larger goals?
- What's the business impact?


## Step 8: Distribute & Follow Up

### Choose Distribution Method

**Email**:
- Good for formal, comprehensive updates
- Easy to forward and archive
- Can include attachments
- Trackable (read receipts)

**Slack/Teams**:
- Good for quick, informal updates
- High visibility in active channels
- Easy discussion and questions
- May get buried in noise

**Document (Notion, Confluence, Docs)**:
- Good for living documents
- Can be updated in place
- Easy to find historical updates
- Requires people to check proactively

**Presentation**:
- Good for monthly/quarterly reviews
- Formal settings
- Allows discussion
- More time-intensive

**Best practice**: Combination
- Post in primary channel (e.g., Slack)
- Link to full document for details
- Email if decisions are needed
- Present in recurring meetings

### Timing

**Best times to send**:
- **Monday morning**: Set context for the week
- **Friday afternoon**: Reflect on week's progress
- **Before key meetings**: Prime discussion

**Avoid**:
- Late Friday (gets lost over weekend)
- During holidays
- Right before major deadlines (won't be read)

### Encourage Engagement

**Make it easy to respond**:
- Ask specific questions
- Use polls for decisions
- Request feedback on specific topics
- Provide multiple ways to engage (comment, DM, meeting)

**Example**:
> "💬 Question for stakeholders: Should we prioritize feature A (faster to ship) or feature B (higher user value)? React with 👍 for A or ❤️ for B, or comment with thoughts."


## Step 9: Track & Measure

### Track Action Items

**Maintain a list**:
- Action from update
- Owner
- Deadline
- Status
- Notes

**Follow up**:
- Check in before deadline
- Escalate if at risk
- Acknowledge completion
- Report on outcomes in next update

### Measure Effectiveness

**Good signs your updates are working**:
- Stakeholders reference them in meetings
- You receive relevant, timely feedback
- Blockers get unblocked quickly
- Fewer ad-hoc status requests
- Decisions happen on schedule
- People engage (comments, questions)

**Signs to improve**:
- Low engagement (no responses)
- Still getting lots of "what's the status?" questions
- Decisions getting delayed
- Surprises in meetings ("I didn't know that!")
- Stakeholder confusion about priorities

### Solicit Feedback

**Periodically ask**:
- Is this update useful?
- What information is missing?
- What could be clearer?
- Is the frequency right?
- Is the format working?


## Step 10: Maintain Consistency

### Create a Routine

**Block time weekly/monthly** for:
- Gathering information (30 min)
- Drafting update (30-60 min)
- Reviewing and editing (15 min)
- Distributing and following up (15 min)

### Use Templates

**Benefits**:
- Saves time
- Ensures consistency
- Nothing gets forgotten
- Easy for readers to scan

**Customize templates**:
- Adjust sections for your project
- Add project-specific metrics
- Remove irrelevant parts

### Build a Library

**Save your updates**:
- Easy to reference history
- Show progress over time
- Useful for performance reviews
- Helpful for new team members
- Template for future projects


## Tips for Great Stakeholder Updates

### ✅ Do's

**Be proactive**: Share bad news quickly, don't let it surprise stakeholders

**Be honest**: Transparency builds trust; sugarcoating erodes it

**Be specific**: "API integration delayed 2 weeks due to security review" not "some delays"

**Be forward-looking**: Balance reporting past with planning future

**Be consistent**: Regular cadence builds trust and expectations

**Be actionable**: Every update should enable decisions or actions

**Celebrate wins**: Recognize team and progress; not everything is problems

### ❌ Don'ts

**Don't wait for perfection**: Better to share draft than delay update

**Don't bury the lead**: Most important info first

**Don't assume context**: Brief reminder of what project is about

**Don't surprise people**: Major changes shouldn't first appear in written update

**Don't make excuses**: Own problems and focus on solutions

**Don't be vague**: Specifics over generalities

**Don't forget follow-up**: Track what you commit to do

**For CPO/COO-facing updates or reports**: Consider running the [Document Review Panel](document-review-panel.md) (PM, Engineering, Ops, CPO, COO personas) to catch gaps and sharpen the narrative before sending.


## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Issue: No one reads my updates

**Solutions**:
- Make TL;DR really clear (5 seconds to understand)
- Shorten overall update (aim for < 5 min read)
- Use more visual elements (emojis, tables, bullets)
- Send at better time (not late Friday)
- Lead with what matters to them (not what matters to you)
- Ask engaging questions

### Issue: Still getting lots of status questions

**Solutions**:
- Update more frequently
- Answer questions preemptively in update
- Link to dashboards for real-time status
- Be more specific about timeline and next steps
- Make sure update reaches all stakeholders

### Issue: Decisions get stuck

**Solutions**:
- Call out decisions more explicitly
- Set clear deadline for decision
- Provide recommendation, not just options
- Explain impact of delay
- Follow up directly with decision-maker
- Escalate if needed

### Issue: Updates take too long to write

**Solutions**:
- Use template consistently
- Gather info throughout period (not all at end)
- Set time limit (45 min max)
- Focus on essentials, cut the rest
- Reuse from previous update (trends, not just snapshots)
- Use bullet points instead of paragraphs
